FIELD ETHICS & ECOSYSTEM SAFETY

Subtropical Marine & Wetland Safety Protocols

Safeguarding both travelers and fragile national park ecosystems: alligator vs. crocodile behavior, mineral reef sunscreen, and mosquito defense.

WILDLIFE PROTOCOLS

Alligators vs. American Crocodiles

South Florida is the only place on Earth where native American Alligators (freshwater sawgrass sloughs) and endangered American Crocodiles (coastal saltwater mangroves) coexist:

AMERICAN ALLIGATOR
Broad U-Shaped Snout
Dark black/grey. Plentiful along Shark Valley tram trail and Anhinga boardwalk.
AMERICAN CROCODILE
Narrow V-Shaped Snout
Olive-tan coloration. Basks openly at Flamingo Marina basin and Florida Bay docks.
  • The 15-Foot Buffer: Federal park law requires maintaining a minimum 15-foot (4.5 m) distance from all crocodilians at all times. Never feed or corner wildlife.
  • Trail Mindfulness: At Shark Valley, alligators frequently sunbathe directly on the edge of the asphalt bike/tram trail. Remain calmly centered on the roadway.
MARINE REEF ETHICS

Reef Sunscreen & Snorkel Stewardship

Biscayne and Dry Tortugas harbor some of the most delicate living coral heads remaining in the Florida Reef Tract:

MINERAL ZINC ONLY: Chemical sunscreens containing oxybenzone or octinoxate cause coral bleaching and DNA damage. Bring non-nano Zinc Oxide (20%+) reef-safe sunscreen.
  • Never Touch Coral: Corals are delicate living colonial animals. Touching or standing on coral heads breaks decades of growth and causes infectious bacterial die-offs.
  • No Shiny Jewelry: Remove glittering watches, rings, and chains before snorkeling. Flashy silver jewelry can trigger investigative strikes from predatory barracuda.
  • Mangrove Insect Protocol: Bring Picaridin or 30% DEET repellent for dawn/dusk walks on the Snake Bight and Christian Point trails in southern Everglades.
